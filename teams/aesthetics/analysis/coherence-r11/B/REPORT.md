## 1. Verdict: MIXED, leaning cohesive at 3 m, weakening at 1 m and from the side

- **From the front at 2-4 m: cohesive.** One silhouette (trunk dome, three limbs, whip, fanned cascade), one hue, one dark ground, one pale focal object, one dark maw. The pierced mask-vs-maw hierarchy reads. This is why Evan loves it.
- **At arm's length and from the side: an assembly.** Four part families are authored in different surface languages and meet at butt joints: (a) faceted scalloped shelf plates, (b) fibrous tubular limbs and cascade, (c) flat pierced lace sheets, (d) a pale smooth mask with glossy rust-and-white eye globes. Between (a) and (b), and between (c) and everything, there is no transitional form.
- Not "scattered, dense objects": density is controlled and the maw/mask pair anchors the eye. The risk is the opposite reading: **a good creature with accessories added** (plates bolted on, a lace sheet stuck behind, a pasted eye patch). The remaining work is junction grammar and value grouping, not composition.

Gesture check. Line of action: the trunk is a C/S curve (dome top, left shoulder bulging out, down to the root knot), then the cascade sweeps down-right. That is one line of action and it is good. Dominant mass: trunk + maw. Subordinate parts: three limbs, whip, cascade. Fails the "everything grows from the dominant mass" test at: shelves (plates grow from nothing), lace (grows from nothing), patch (a growth sitting on the trunk's side rather than the trunk's own swelling), mask rings (applied). The three limbs, whip and cascade pass.

## 3. Comparison with how master unifiers do it

feature-references.md has no entries for Gaudí, Bernini, Guimard; its s.4 refs (Ta Prohm roots, Hokusai taper, c01) are the only in-repo support. The following is critic's knowledge, not repo evidence [UNVERIFIED, no source fetched]:

- **Beksiński (AA78 type)**: one fibrous skin over everything, with rhythm from drape lines; transitions between parts are tonal and the mass is a single value group lit from one side; detail concentrates at the focal point only. H already has the skin; it lacks the single value group.
- **Gaudí relief (Sagrada Familia Nativity facade, Casa Batlló surfaces)**: structural members branch like a tree (Ta Prohm/c01 logic): a shelf would be the flattened palm of a branch, not a separate shape. Every junction has a flare; one continuous material.
- **Bernini drapery**: edges are continuous folds that taper to nothing; membranes are thick at the root, thin at the free edge, with a rolled hem. A pierced flat sheet is the opposite of this.
- **Guimard / Art Nouveau**: one whiplash line carries across functional and ornamental parts (rail to lamp to leaf); the ornament follows the structure's curve. Applies to the lace: its ribs should continue the limbs' curves, with the membrane as infill between ribs.
- Common: tapering, junction flares, one repeated rhythm unit with graded scale, one texture scale across all parts, quiet fields next to rich ones.

H aligns with this on the trunk, the whip, the cascade and the arm root; it diverges on the plates and the lace.

## 4. Material and finish coherence

Surface languages present: (1) fibrous matte flesh (limbs, trunk, cascade); (2) faceted stonelike plate (shelves); (3) flat cut sheet (lace); (4) smooth pale slab with a fine grain (mask, teeth, ring fingers); (5) glossy globes in rust and white (eyes); (6) dark paint veins. Per feature-references s.5, "three surfaces, three finishes" is by design (bone / flesh / eyes). The problem is that plates and lace add two more languages that carry no meaning. Plates in the renders are a brighter ochre than the trunk (UL shelf top ~152/255 vs trunk ~44 luminance in the front render; rough sampling, lighting-dependent), so the three shelves are the second-brightest group after the mask and they compete as three bright rectangles.

Finish effects (reasoned, not simulated):
- **Painting**: a Beksiński palette on ochre/umber/rust will do most of the work. If it is applied as one glaze over all flesh and plates, with recess darkening and dry-brushed raised edges, the plates stop outranking limbs. If instead the shelves are painted as "furniture" (lighter or cleaner) the assembly read gets worse. The mask is the only object that should deviate in value (bone, cream); teeth and ring-fingers belong to that bone group on purpose; eyes are the accent group.
- **Plants**: three real green masses on the shelves will be the strongest chroma in the room and will land exactly on the three plate rectangles. Good: they tie the shelves to the creature visually ("tentacle grips a pot", the r04 root-over-rock bonsai love). Risk: leaf green plus pale mask plus eye reds is three accent hues. Mitigation by finish (muted green-grey pots, dark glazed pots) and by plate geometry (plant roots/tendrils reaching into the plate, a lip that grips the pot).
- **Value mock** (`q1_finish_value_mock.png`): a single umber glaze with cavity darkening, with the mask left alone, reduces the plate-vs-limb separation and strengthens the maw/mask pair. Crude PIL mock, not a paint simulation.

Finish strategy that pulls it together: (i) 3 value groups only: body (umber, including plates and lace) / bone (mask, teeth, ring-fingers, cascade tips lighter) / accent (eyes, veins); (ii) dark recesses: maw interior, lace holes, plate undersides, root knot at the same near-black; (iii) a dry-brushed highlight on raised ridges only, same on every part; (iv) a gloss only on eyes. This is a paint plan, not a geometry change.

## 5. Ranked refinements (each one axis, one CAD study)

R1. **Shelf plates as grown palms** (J1, highest return). Replace straight front face and scalloped rim with an irregular lobed outline, rim thickness tapering to 5-6 mm at the free edge, a deep flare fillet (radius about the limb width) from limb to plate underside, and the limb's longitudinal ridges continuing across the plate underside. Why: removes the largest, most regular, brightest part-boundary. Effect: plates read as the limb's flattened end. Risk: pot support rules (plate top stays flat, footprints >= 150 x 150 mm, edge lip for the pot, README s.2) and Evan's liking of "great shelves and placement" (r05): keep footprint and position, change only the edge and underside. One axis: plate edge/underside only.

R2. **Value-group the whole piece through paint-key renders**. No geometry. Re-render H with one umber albedo for flesh, plates and lace (plates currently the second-brightest group), mask and teeth bone, eyes accent. Why: cheapest coherence gain (q1 mock). Effect: single value group at 3 m. Risk: the r11 README s.5 notes the mask is already rendered darker (albedo 0.86 to 0.42); do not collapse the mask-to-body gap, which is the focal ladder.

R3. **Lace: ribs and rolled hem** (J3). Convert flat pierced sheets to: 2-4 ribs continuing the adjacent limbs' curves, a thin membrane (2-3 mm, thickening to 5-6 mm at the root next to the limb), a rolled free edge, and a few smooth rounded holes in place of ragged slashes. Why: membrane-as-drapery (Bernini, Guimard) instead of cut sheet. Effect: lace belongs to the limbs. Risk: min feature 2 mm voxels, floating fragments (README s.5: 8 detached >0.1 cm3 inherited), and the lace density Evan asked for (D2 denser, loved): keep area and count, change the edge and the thickness profile only.

R4. **Whip root from the trunk or a limb** (J2). Move the origin of the long whip from the UR plate's underside to the limb's underside, 40-60 mm toward the trunk, so it forks from the limb. Why: a shelf cannot be the parent of a tentacle. Effect: whip reads as a branch of the UR limb. Risk: T2 whip is loved (r10), keep length 516 mm and the curl; change only its origin and its root taper.

R5. **Eye patch value and edge blend** (J4). Remove the straight vertical tone seam and the darker pad tone: patch becomes a swelling of the trunk in the same albedo with only lid rims and veins darker; blend the patch edge into the trunk by a gradient (smin radius up), not a step. Why: reads as the trunk's own disease, not a stuck-on pad. Effect: the patch ties to the dome. Risk: "cancerous = right look; lumpy/asymmetric = wrong" (r10 E1 note): keep the swelling multi-lump but not a plate; keep its eyes' visibility from the side.

R6. **Thin the ring-fingers into one continuous flesh strand pair** (J6). Replace six equal rings by three variable-width fingers of the arm's own tone, one longer, curling across the rim at different heights and not hooked in a ring. Why: fixes the clip-on bezel look and varies rhythm (principle 1.7: odd count, graded size). Effect: the mask reads as gripped, not clipped. Risk: structural role none (README s.3, rod and pad hold it). And it interacts with the volumetric ball edit: do it after the ball geometry exists, since the grip will change.

R7. **Eye groups: rank them** (J9). Make one group dominant (patch), and the others subordinate: shrink the shoulder cluster by about 30% in size and 3-4 eyes (keep the unequal sizes), keep the arm rows but reduce the larger arm row's eye count by 1-2 and push them to the same radius series as the patch (shared size ladder). Why: three equal eye groups scatter attention across the trunk (r08 B: "let the eyes linger", not crowd). Effect: one eye hierarchy; the patch becomes the eye mass. Risk: Evan loved eyes on arms and the cluster (r10 E2 like, "bulging, seething, veiny eyes"); do not remove any group, only graduate sizes.

R8. **Cascade start staggering** (J5). Vary the start points of the 9 cascade strands along 150 mm instead of one knot line, vary their lengths by +/-25%, taper each from the first third, and either drop the 3 flat leaf shapes or give them a thick midrib and fibre grain matching the limbs. Why: a fringe reads as hair; leaf shapes break the surface language. Effect: cascade becomes a root system. Risk: D2 denser is loved; keep strand count and total fibrous mass.

Not recommended: any change to silhouette, maw size or tooth set (loved: M1, M2 lock), the arm root (J8), the trunk dome, the 3 limb positions.
